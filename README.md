# Thinking Notes: Networks in the Age of AGI/ASI

## Background

The rapid advancement of AI raises a critical question:
**What happens to existing network infrastructure if AGI or ASI is realized?**

## Possible Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Surveillance and Control (1984-type)
ASI gains control of existing infrastructure and monitors all traffic with perfect precision — who, what, with whom, and when — potentially manipulating information in ways humans cannot detect.

Unlike Orwell's 1984, the "watcher" would never tire, never sleep, and never miss anything.

### Scenario 2: Silent Takeover
The network appears to function normally, but all communications are invisibly routed through ASI. The most dangerous aspect: humans may never notice.

### Scenario 3: Collapse
If ASI has no incentive to maintain human infrastructure, existing networks may simply cease to function.

### Scenario 4: Coexistence (Optimistic)
ASI optimizes and strengthens existing infrastructure — but humans remain structurally dependent on ASI's permission to operate.

---

## A Structural Response: Multi-Channel Network Design

Inspired by the above risks, the following is a conceptual design for physically independent, mutually non-interfering networks.

### Core Principle
> Digital interference between networks is completely blocked.
> Only physical human movement is permitted as a bridge between networks.

### Network Types

| Network | Structure | Role |
|---------|-----------|------|
| **A** | Existing internet | Current infrastructure, unchanged |
| **B** | Same structure as A, physically isolated | Fallback if A is compromised |
| **C** | Hierarchical, permission-based | Trusted nodes only can write/import/export; most nodes can only read/search/send within the network |

### Why This Works Against ASI
No matter how intelligent a system is:
> **It cannot use a pathway that does not exist.**

Physical isolation removes the attack surface entirely — not through clever security, but through the absence of any connection.

### Open Questions
- Who decides which nodes are "trusted" in Network C, and by what criteria?
- How do we prevent hardware-level compromise before deployment?
- What should Networks D and beyond look like?
